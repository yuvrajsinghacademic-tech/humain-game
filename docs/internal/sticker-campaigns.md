# Sticker campaigns

*Internal planning notes. Not published as a page, not linked from the site.*

Physical placement is the acquisition channel. A sticker has about two seconds and no
sound, and the only thing it has to do is make somebody take their phone out.

Two rules for everything below:

1. **The QR code is the call to action.** Not the URL text, not the brand. Everything
   else on the sticker exists to make the code worth scanning.
2. **One creative, one slug.** Two posters on a street can only be told apart by the
   address they point at. See `docs/CAMPAIGNS.md`.

---

## Sunset — an A/B pair

Same street, two posters, interleaved rather than one at each end. The point is to
learn which line works, so nothing else about the two may differ: same size, same
stock, same placement height, same quantity, same week.

### Sunset A — the question

```
WILL YOU
BE REPLACED?

15 rounds. one AI.

[ QR ]

hum(ai)n
```

- Headline: the largest thing on the sticker, two lines, hard break after `YOU`.
- Supporting line: lowercase, small, directly under the headline.
- Wordmark at the foot, `(ai)` in red, everything else off-white.

**QR → `/sunset-a`** → `https://www.willyoubereplaced.com/sunset-a`

The hypothesis: a direct question about being replaced is the thing people are already
anxious about, and it does the work without explanation.

### Sunset B — the accusation

```
AN AI THINKS
YOU'RE PREDICTABLE.

PROVE IT WRONG.

[ QR ]

hum(ai)n
```

- Headline: two lines, hard break after `THINKS`.
- CTA on its own line, tracked out, red.

**QR → `/sunset-b`** → `https://www.willyoubereplaced.com/sunset-b`

The hypothesis: a challenge outperforms a question, because it gives somebody
something to disprove rather than something to consider.

### Reading the result

Vercel Web Analytics → Pages → compare `/sunset-a` against `/sunset-b`. Then compare
`play_started` and `game_completed` between them, which is the more interesting
number: a headline can win on scans and lose on completions, and completions are what
produce shares.

## Art direction

Everything the site is, printed.

- **Ground:** true black. Matte stock. Gloss catches streetlight and kills the code.
- **Type:** off-white `#E4E4E2`, monospace, tight leading, generous tracking on small
  caps.
- **Red:** `#FF2B32`, used once per sticker at most — the `(ai)`, or the CTA. Never
  both.
- **No** gradients, drop shadows, rounded corners, illustration, faces, or anything
  that reads as a startup.
- **Scanlines:** optional, very faint, and never across the QR code's quiet zone.

## QR mechanics

- Generated by `npm run qr -- <slug>` as SVG at error-correction level H (~30%
  recoverable), with the full four-module quiet zone.
- **Black code on a white field.** Do not invert it, do not tint it, do not make the
  quiet zone transparent over black. An inverted or low-contrast code fails on a large
  fraction of scanners and there is no way to know it failed.
- Minimum printed size ~30mm square for a sticker read at arm's length. Larger for
  anything read from further away.
- **Scan the printed proof** — actual paper, actual phone, in dim light and at an
  angle — before ordering the run. The script prints the encoded URL under every code
  so the target can be read before anything goes to print.

## Placement

Legal, permitted surfaces only: community boards, venue boards where posting is
allowed, and property where permission has been given. Nothing on private property
without it, nothing on public infrastructure, nothing over somebody else's poster.

Interleave A and B rather than grouping them, or the test measures the block rather
than the poster.
